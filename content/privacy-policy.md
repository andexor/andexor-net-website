## Executive Summary

This is how we handle personal information. In short, three separate things happen here, and each has its own switch.

- If you visit the Site, the cookie banner decides whether analytics and advertising run in your browser.
- If you contact us, we use what you sent to reply to you.
- If we contact your business first, we found your details from a data provider or a public source. The cookie banner had nothing to do with it and the one-click unsubscribe link or a reply is how you stop it.

## 1. Who We Are

Andexor Network, Inc. ("Andexor," "we," "us," or "our") operates https://andexor.net/, including every page under that address (the "Site"). This Privacy Policy explains how we handle personal information. It is incorporated into our [Terms of Service](/terms-of-service), and words defined there have the same meaning here. Sections 20 through 25 explain how we use cookies and similar technologies on the Site.

Andexor is the business, or "controller," responsible for the personal information this policy covers. Our contact details are in Section 19.

## 2. What This Policy Covers

By "personal information" we mean information that identifies, relates to, or could reasonably be linked to a particular person or household. Information that has been aggregated or de-identified so that it can no longer be linked to a person is not personal information.

This policy covers personal information about:

- visitors to the Site;
- people who contact us, through the Contact Us form, by email, by telephone, or by mail;
- client contacts, meaning the people who represent a business that has engaged us;
- prospects, meaning people whose contact details we obtained from a source other than the person, as described in Section 6; and
- security researchers who report an issue under our [Security Policy](/security-policy).

## 3. What This Policy Does Not Cover

When we perform services for a client, we may handle personal information that belongs to the client, such as the visitors, subscribers, and customers of the client's own websites and other digital properties. We handle that information on the client's behalf and on its instructions, as its service provider or processor, under the Andexor Network, Inc. Services Agreement. The client's own privacy policy governs it. If you have a question or a request about that information, contact the client, and we will help the client respond.

## 4. Information We Collect

### Information You Give Us

- **Inquiry details.** When you submit the Contact Us form or otherwise contact us, we collect what you send: typically your name, email address, telephone number, company name, website address, and the content of your message.
- **Information about your website and business.** If you ask for an audit, proposal, or quote, we collect and review information about your website and other digital properties in order to prepare it.
- **Client account and billing details.** If you engage us, we collect the names and contact details of your designated contacts, your billing details, and records of our dealings with you. Payments are handled by our payment processor; we do not store full payment card numbers.
- **Feedback.** Any ratings, reviews, testimonials, or other Feedback you give us, as described in the Terms of Service.
- **Security reports.** If you report a security issue, we collect what the Security Policy asks you to include: your contact details, a description of the issue, the date and time of your testing, the Internet Protocol (IP) address you tested from, any accounts or usernames you used, and how you would like to be credited.

### Information Collected Automatically

- **Server and security logs.** Like most websites, our servers and our hosting and security providers record each request: IP address, date and time, the page requested, the referring page, the browser or agent identifier ("user agent"), and the response. This applies to automated agents and crawlers as well as to people.
- **Device and usage information.** With your permission, the analytics and advertising technologies described in Section 5 collect information such as cookie identifiers, pages viewed, links clicked, time on page, approximate location derived from your IP address (city or region, not a precise location), device type, browser, operating system, and how you arrived at the Site.

### Information From Other Sources

- **Lead Data.** We obtain business contact and firmographic information from third-party data providers and public sources. See Section 6.
- **Service providers and partners.** We receive reports from our analytics and advertising providers about how visitors use the Site and respond to our advertising.
- **Public sources.** We may review publicly available information about your business, such as your website, business listings, and public social media profiles, when you ask us about our services or when we discover your business, website, or social profiles and look for opportunities to serve you.

**Information we do not want.** We do not ask for, and ask you not to send us, sensitive personal information: government identifiers, financial account or payment card numbers, account passwords, health information, precise geolocation, biometric or genetic information, or information about a person's race, ethnicity, religion, politics, union membership, sex life, sexual orientation, citizenship, or immigration status. We do not use or disclose sensitive personal information to infer characteristics about anyone.

You are not required by law to give us any information. If you choose not to, we may be unable to answer your inquiry or prepare what you asked for.

A cookie is a small file that a website stores in your browser. A pixel is a small piece of code that tells a third party that a page was loaded or an action was taken. We use three kinds of these technologies. Sections 20 through 25 explain them in more detail, and the [Cookie Declaration](#25-cookie-declaration) in Section 25 lists every cookie the Site uses and lets you change your choice.

Meta uses this information to report results to us, to let us show our advertisements to people who have visited the Site and to people similar to them, and for its own purposes described in the [Meta Privacy Policy](https://www.facebook.com/privacy/policy). **Your choice comes first.** Analytics and advertising technologies stay off until you allow them in the cookie banner. You can change your choice at any time through the **Privacy Settings** link in the footer of every page, which opens the cookie banner again, or the **Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information** link beside the copyright line, which opens the [Cookie Declaration](#25-cookie-declaration) in Section 25. **What the cookie banner does not do.** The cookie banner controls only the analytics and advertising technologies on this Site. It does not identify you, and declining it does not tell us who you are. It does not cause and cannot stop an email or telephone call from us. If we contact your business and you have never dealt with us, it is because we found your business contact details as Section 6 describes, not because of anything you did or did not do on the Site. If you received a marketing email and do not wish to receive more, use the one-click unsubscribe link in the email. If you received a marketing phone call and do not wish to be called again, please tell us on the call. Or you may write to us, as Section 11 describes.

**Global Privacy Control.** If your browser or an extension sends a Global Privacy Control (GPC) signal, we treat it as a request from that browser to opt out of the sale and sharing of personal information and of targeted advertising, and advertising technologies stay off for that browser.

Your browser's settings also let you block or delete cookies. If you delete cookies, we will ask for your choice again on your next visit.

## 6. Lead Data

This Section explains how we handle information about people who have not dealt with us directly.

### What it is

"Lead Data" is business contact and firmographic information that we buy or license from third-party data providers, or gather from public sources such as company websites, business directories, and public professional profiles. It typically includes a person's name, employer, job title, business email address, business telephone number, business address, professional profile address, and information about the business itself, such as its industry, size, location, and website. We also create simple inferences from it, such as whether a business is likely to be a good fit for a service. Lead Data has no connection to your visit to the Site or to your cookie choices.

### What it is not

We do not buy or use Lead Data that includes sensitive personal information of the kinds listed in Section 4, information about anyone we know to be under 18, or precise geolocation. We do not knowingly buy Lead Data about people located in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland, California, Oregon, Texas, or Vermont. We do not deliver to clients Lead Data about people or businesses located in California, Oregon, Texas, or Vermont, and we are not registered as a data broker in any state. We determine location from the business address and other location information in the record.

Lead Data is not a "consumer report." We do not provide it, and our clients may not use it, to decide anyone's eligibility for credit, insurance, employment, housing, or any similar purpose.

## 12. Your Privacy Rights

Privacy laws in a growing number of states give their residents rights over their personal information. These include California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Maryland, Minnesota, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, Oregon, Rhode Island, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, and Virginia, among others. Rather than treat people differently by state, we offer the rights below to every resident of the United States, whether or not a particular law applies to us or to you.

You may ask us to:

- confirm whether we hold personal information about you, and give you access to it;
- tell you the categories of sources it came from, and the third parties to whom we disclosed it;
- correct it if it is inaccurate;
- delete it;
- give you a portable copy of the information you provided to us;
- opt out of the sale or sharing of your personal information, of targeted advertising, and of profiling that produces legal or similarly significant effects (we do not engage in that kind of profiling); and
- appeal a decision we make about your request.

**How to make a request.** Write to privacy@andexor.net, call 855-ANDEXOR, or write to the mailing address in Section 19. To opt out of advertising technologies, you can also use the **Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information** link or a Global Privacy Control signal; those do not require you to identify yourself.

**How we verify.** To protect you, we need to confirm that a request to access, correct, or delete comes from the person the information is about. We usually do that by replying to the email address we have on file for you, and we may ask for a small amount of additional information if what we hold is sensitive. We use what you give us for verification only. We do not verify opt-out requests.

**Authorized agents.** You may have someone make a request for you. We will ask the agent for proof of your signed permission, and we may ask you to confirm it with us directly.

**When we respond.** We complete most requests within a few business days, and in every case within 30 days. We act on opt-out requests as soon as we reasonably can, and never later than 15 business days.

**Appeals.** If we decline your request, we will say why. You may appeal by replying to our decision within 60 days. We will take a fresh look at the request and at our reasons, and answer in writing within 30 days. If you are still not satisfied, you may complain to the attorney general of your state.

**No retaliation.** We will not deny you service, charge you a different price, or treat you worse because you used any of these rights. We do not offer financial incentives in exchange for personal information.

**Limits.** These rights are not absolute. We may keep information that we need to complete a transaction you requested, to detect and investigate security incidents, to comply with the law, or to defend a legal claim, and we keep a suppression record after an opt-out so that the opt-out keeps working. In practice, the reasons we might not do all of what you ask are these:

- We cannot confirm that the request comes from the person the information is about. We will tell you what we need, and we act as soon as you provide it.
- The records are client billing records that we must keep for tax and accounting purposes, as the retention periods in Section 9 describe. We delete everything else and tell you what we kept.
- The record is your entry on our suppression list. Keeping it is what prevents your information from coming back to us from another source, so we keep the minimum needed for that purpose, as Section 6 describes.
- The records are tied to an open security investigation or legal dispute. We act when it is closed.
- The information belongs to a client and we hold it only as the client's service provider, as Section 3 describes. We direct you to the client and help the client respond.
- Giving you a copy of a record would also disclose another person's information. We give you what is yours and withhold the rest.

In every case, we will tell you which of these applies and what we did with the rest of your request.

## 21. What Cookies Are

A cookie is a small text file that a website stores in your browser. Similar technologies do the same kind of job: pixels, tags, scripts, and the browser's local and session storage. We call all of them "cookies" in this policy.

Websites use cookies to keep the site working, to remember choices, to measure how the site is used, and to measure advertising. Blocking the ones a site needs can break parts of it.

## 22. The Four Cookie Categories
