# Technical SEO

Make your content readable. Make it discoverable. Make it explode.

## What is an exact match domain (EMD)?

One of the most important things a business owner can do for SEO
is to have a domain name that matches their business name
and use it everywhere.
Variations and distortions of the name across the Internet tend to
weaken your brand, distract potential buyers,
and keep you hidden in searches as well as agentic conversations.
Using a consistent brand name strategy in your website,
email address, newsletters, and social media accounts
forms a solid foundation on which to work and be seen.

## How can I obtain brand authority across the Internet?

For businesses that were founded before the Internet
and businesses that adopted social media slowly,
consistency may be more difficult to obtain.
The best domain name for your business may already be taken.
No worries. There are plenty of alternative
top-level domain names (TLDs) you can use.

The best social media handle for your business
may already be taken by another business with
the same name in another state.
You may end up with a variety of names
fragmented across multiple platforms.

If you must keep all of the various names

You may need to pick a new name that isn't
being used anywhere and start using it everywhere.

## What if I have a page that is crawled, but not indexed?

This is a common issue where Google found a page of yours,
but chose not to list it.

In some cases, this is temporary and it moves on
to appearing in search results soon enough.

When that does not happen, it could mean that
the page has no incoming links pointing to it,
so it appears to have no value yet.
If so, then driving more traffic to the page
from other sites and links from social media posts
can help Google to decide that it has value and should be listed.

It can also mean that the content is generic
and does not offer anything new that it hasn't
already seen from a thousand other sites.
If that is the case, you may want to spice up the copywriting a bit.
Give it a new angle or new perspective that was not clear before.

Another simple thing is that it helps when the
URL, title, H1, and description all say the same thing.
They don't have to be an exact, word-for-word match,
but it helps.

## Ongoing Support

For some pages and sites, information does not change very often.
Get a page setup correctly the first time, and it can rank high
for a long time, though not forever.

But when pages are created or updated frequently
or new products and services are announced occasionally,
an ongoing review is really necessary.

There are so many aspects to good search engine optimisation.
Everything can not be done all at once.
Some things require a bit of time for trial and error.
A/B testing can sometimes reveal whether an assumption is true or not.
Some techniques are meant for your website, while others
belong to social media, blogs, newsletters, maps, and other sources.
Some require extra time to reach past a threshold
to prove that you have domain authority
or topical authority.

Plus, if you make too many changes all at once,
one of two known outcomes await you.
All of your changes may get ignored.
Or some may have a huge impact while others
are negligible and there is no way to know which
ones made the difference.

From time to time, Google publishes new guidance on
what works and what doesn't work.
Techniques that used to work last year may not work anymore.
You add, change, and remove products and services.
And so do your competitors.
You could rank high for a certain word or phrase for some time,
only to be suddenly knocked down by a competitor with a similar product
or by paid ads or even an AI summary.

For all of these reasons, SEO is not 
a one-time set-it-and-forget it type of activity.
There are always things happening outside of your control.
There are always new experiments to run
and new techniques to try.
