# Cost Reduction

Do what you do. Spend less.

## Database

Some databases are configured with
more capacity than they really need
and that costs a lot of money to maintain.
We can take a look at your configuration
and recommend whether to downgrade
compute or storage resources.

In an effort to keep a database management system
in a maintainable state and keep the number of
security vulnerabilities low, cloud vendors
tend to require an aggressive regiment of
upgrades in database software.
Remaining on an old version for legacy system
compatibility often comes with an additional cost.
Let us help you with routine migrations
to keep these costs low.

We can also take an inventory of your
database schema and identify and remove
any data that is not being used.

Likewise, when data is no longer needed
for day-to-day operations, we can
help you archive it and even setup
automated tasks for routine archival.

## Application

Most applications today are either
underpowered or overpowered.
We can examine usage metrics
and recommend configuration changes
to scale up or down automatically as needed.
There are a lot more options available
that what comes with default settings.

In some cases, this can be a long road,
as optimizations for one service
may not be realized until an entire
cluster can be scaled down.
We can find out if that applies to you.

## Logs

Every application handles logs in one of two ways:
they either log way too much information or not enough.
In our experience, it is usually both at the same time.

Without careful planning during an application's
design phases, developers don't really know what should be logged
and what will never be needed.
This results in situations where errors are happening all day long
and never being logged, while in other parts of the app,
volumes of information is logged that no one will ever need.

We know how to analyze both scenarios and tame them
so you will have only what you need, when you need it.
This not only makes applications far easier to maintain,
it costs less too.

## Metrics

Applications also tend to put a huge amount of
numerical data in logs that can be handled
much more efficiently as metrics instead.
By converting these log entries into metrics,
we can make the app much more observable
with dashboards and alerts, and do it
at a fraction of the cost.

Keeping large amounts of metric data for a long time
is another source of high costs.
In keeping with scheduled reporting requirements,
this data can be automatically summarized
at regular intervals, such as weekly,
monthly, quarterly, and annually.
Combine that with reports built to use multiple data sources
and you can keep more data for a longer time at a lower cost.

## Alerts

In our experience, alert fatigue is the #1 reason why many technical issues
never get resolved. A system that behaves badly can generate
so many alerts that everyone knows there is no time to deal with them.
This becomes more absurd when you look into some alerts and
find false positives or alerts for things that do not matter.

All of that noise drowns out the signals that really matter.
This doesn't even count the immesurable amount of time
people waste on reading and deleting email alerts,
not to mention the compliance curiosities that surround
unhandled critical alerts.

Ignoring the issue is not a good option.
Let us audit all of the sources of alerts you have.
We can remove anything that does not matter.
We can reconfigure ones that are good, but just
set for an unreasonable threshold.
And for a bonus, we can even identify the
false alarms created by timing mismatches.
Just because something happens every day at the same time
does not automatically mean it is an issue.
It may just be a regularly scheduled batch job
performing critical business tasks.
You'll want your alerts to take that into consideration.

What about the alert audience?
Are alerts being sent to the right people?
Or are too many people getting alerts
for issues they can't do anything about?
Let us help you organize the target audiences
with group lists so everyone gets just the alerts they need,
with none of the noise they don't need.

It's an extra fun day when you get alerts
that can't answer basic questions that
would have made them super helpful.

Take a look at some of the alerts you get
every day or on special (unexpected) occasions.
Can your alerts explain basic information like:

- What app generated this alert?
- Did this happen in production or a test environment?
- What is the acceptable minimum or maximum value?
- What is the value right now?
- What value range have we typically been seeing for the past month?
- Does this tend to happen the same time every day?
- What are the likely causes that would make this happen?

If your alerts can answer questions like those,
then they have the actionable intelligence you need.
If they don't, give us a call. We can help.
